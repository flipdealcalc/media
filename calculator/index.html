1. Cash out of pocket to buy and rehab

Everything you pay before the refinance: down payment, closing, agent fee, points, lender and draw fees, the loan payments while the work gets done, and the property's holding costs. The rehab itself comes from the loan.

2. Cost of each loan over its full life

If you kept the purchase loan to the end of its term instead of refinancing.

3. After the rehab: refinance into a DSCR loan

The DSCR loan pays off the purchase loan. Shows the cash you get back, what stays in the deal, and the total borrowing cost from purchase through the full DSCR term.

4. Equity position

Value minus what is owed against the house, at purchase, after the rehab and after the DSCR refi. For a HELOC the debt sits on your own home, but it is shown the same way so the deals compare.

5. HELOC for the down payment

A HELOC on your home pays the down payment and all closing costs (title, agent fee, points and lender fees), and hard money or a conventional loan covers the rest of the price plus the full rehab. At the DSCR refi, the cash back pays off the HELOC first. Uses the HELOC rate and fees from the loan terms above.

6. Sell instead of refinancing (flip exit)

You keep the purchase loan through the rehab and the sale, then pay it off from the sale proceeds. Uses the holding period from section 3 as months until the sale closes, and the selling costs from section 4.

Worst case uses the stress test from section 6 of the calculator: longer hold, lower ARV and buyer concessions.

  • Hard money commonly funds up to 85–90% of price plus 100% of rehab, capped at 70–75% of ARV, with rehab held back and released in draws after an inspection. Built for a 6–12 month hold; expect an extension fee past the term.
  • Conventional only covers rehab as a renovation loan (Fannie Mae HomeStyle), which pays P&I on the full amount from closing and holds the rehab in escrow. It needs a licensed contractor bid and takes longer to close. Most lenders want 6 months of ownership before a cash-out refi.
  • HELOC is borrowed against your own home, so you only pay interest on what you draw and can pay contractors directly. Your line has to be big enough for the purchase plus rehab. The rate is variable.
  • DSCR lenders size the loan to the rent (DSCR 1.0–1.25), usually want 3–6 months of ownership, and most charge a prepayment penalty for the first 3–5 years.
  • HELOC for the down payment: hard money lenders generally allow it. Conventional loans allow a down payment borrowed against an asset you own, like your home, but the HELOC payment counts in your debt-to-income. Every loan here adds leverage, so watch the total borrowed against price plus rehab.
  • Rates as of Oct 2026: 30-year average 7.32% (investor loans run ~0.6% higher), HELOC average 7.34%, DSCR about 6.75–7.75%, hard money 10–12%.
